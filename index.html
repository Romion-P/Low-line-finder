Add game logs

Paste a player's game log once and every category on your slate fills in from it: Points, Rebounds, Assists, PRA, the two-stat combos, 3-PT Made, Steals, Blocks, Turnovers, Fantasy Score and more. Each card then shows bars for every game.

Basketball-Reference: open the player's game log, choose Share & Export → Get table as CSV, and copy the CSV. ESPN: open the player's Game Log tab and drag-select the table from the header row down. Either one works. Type the player's name below unless your paste has a Player column.

Quickest: run fetch_gamelogs_nba.py on your computer, then click Load gamelogs.json to fill in every NBA player at once.

How the over probability is worked out

1. A recency-weighted average of the player's last games (each older game counts 85% as much as the one after it), and the spread of those games.

2. A normal-curve estimate of the chance the next game lands above the line, given that average and spread.

3. The share of the last 10 games that went over the line.

The two estimates are blended 60/40 and pulled 15% toward 50%, since 10 games is a small sample. Edge is how far the weighted average sits above the line. The tick on each bar marks the per-pick break-even rate for a 2-pick power play at your payout setting.

New marks a line that appeared since your previous import or refresh (it stays for 2 hours). ▼ was 25.5 means the line dropped, which helps an over; ▲ was 23.5 means it was raised.

Sportsbook lines: pick a book with the Compare with buttons and each card shows that book's line for the same player and stat. A green tag like DK 26.5 (+2.0) means PrizePicks' line is 2 lower than DraftKings', which helps an over. All books uses the typical line across sportsbooks.

The model does not know about injuries, minutes changes, matchups or line moves. Treat a flag as a reason to look closer.
